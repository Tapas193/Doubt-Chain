1. Problem Statement Analysis

DoubtChain — Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution Tracker

1. Introduction

Organizations and communities consist of people with different levels of experience, knowledge, skills, and responsibilities. Members frequently encounter doubts, practical challenges, and situations where guidance from someone with relevant experience could help them make better decisions or complete tasks more effectively.

Although the answers to many questions may already exist within the organization, finding the right person, locating relevant information, or obtaining a satisfactory answer is not always straightforward. Knowledge may be distributed across informal conversations, messaging platforms, meetings, documents, and individual experiences.

As a result, members may spend time searching for information, repeatedly ask similar questions, or struggle to obtain guidance from people who have already encountered similar situations.

DoubtChain aims to address this challenge by providing a structured peer-to-peer platform where members can raise doubts, share knowledge and experiences, contribute answers, and track questions until they are resolved.

2. Problem Identification

The primary problem is the difficulty of accessing, sharing, and reusing knowledge that already exists within an organization or community.

People who have previously encountered a challenge often possess practical knowledge about its causes, possible solutions, mistakes to avoid, and lessons learned. However, this knowledge may remain limited to individual conversations or personal networks instead of being accessible to everyone who could benefit from it.

Members seeking help may not know whom to approach, where to look for existing answers, or whether their questions have been addressed previously. Even when someone provides an answer, there may be no consistent mechanism for following up, confirming whether the answer was helpful, or identifying doubts that remain unresolved.

The problem is therefore not simply a lack of information. It is also a challenge of knowledge discovery, peer-to-peer collaboration, information continuity, and resolution tracking.

3. Problem Context

This problem can occur across different types of organizations and communities, including:

Educational institutions: Members seeking guidance about academic activities, projects, events, and institutional processes.

Corporate organizations: Employees needing clarification about workflows, tools, internal procedures, or practical work-related challenges.

Technical communities: Developers and technical professionals seeking solutions, implementation guidance, and lessons from others' experiences.

Volunteer and professional communities: Members coordinating activities, sharing operational knowledge, and learning from previous initiatives.

Project teams: Team members requiring clarification about responsibilities, decisions, technical issues, and established practices.

Although the questions differ across these environments, the underlying challenge remains similar: relevant knowledge may exist within the community but may not be easy to find, access, or reuse.

4. Target Users and Stakeholders

4.1 Members seeking guidance

These are individuals who encounter questions, uncertainties, or practical challenges and need assistance from people with relevant knowledge or experience.

Their needs include:

Asking questions in a structured and accessible way.

Finding previously answered questions.

Identifying people who can provide relevant guidance.

Receiving clear and useful responses.

Tracking the progress of their questions.

Confirming whether their doubts have been resolved.

4.2 Experienced members and contributors

These are individuals who possess relevant expertise, knowledge, or firsthand experience and can help others overcome similar challenges.

Their contributions may include:

Sharing practical experiences and lessons learned.

Explaining procedures or technical concepts.

Suggesting solutions to recurring problems.

Answering questions from other members.

Preserving useful knowledge for future reference.

4.3 Organization administrators and community moderators

These stakeholders may help maintain a useful and organized knowledge-sharing environment.

Their potential responsibilities include managing inappropriate content, maintaining community guidelines, organizing categories, and encouraging constructive participation.

4.4 Organizational leadership

Depending on the use case, organizational leaders may benefit from understanding recurring questions, common knowledge gaps, and areas where members frequently require assistance. Such insights could help improve documentation, training, and internal processes.

5. Root Cause Analysis

The following are potential causes of the problem that should be validated through user research.

5.1 Fragmented knowledge

Information may be distributed across multiple communication channels, documents, meetings, and personal conversations. This makes it difficult to locate the most relevant information when needed.

5.2 Dependence on personal networks

Members may rely on knowing the right person or having direct access to someone experienced. People outside those networks may find it harder to obtain the same guidance.

5.3 Repetition of similar questions

When previous questions and answers are difficult to discover, members may repeatedly ask questions that have already been addressed.

5.4 Limited preservation of practical experience

Valuable lessons learned through real situations may remain undocumented or become difficult to retrieve over time. Consequently, future members may not benefit from previous experiences.

5.5 Lack of resolution visibility

In informal discussions, it may be unclear whether a question has received a satisfactory answer, still requires assistance, or has been abandoned without resolution.

5.6 Barriers to participation

Some members may hesitate to ask questions because they fear judgment, feel uncomfortable approaching experienced individuals, or do not know the appropriate channel for seeking help.

5.7 Difficulty identifying recurring knowledge gaps

Without an organized record of questions, an organization may find it difficult to recognize which topics repeatedly create confusion or require better documentation.

6. Impact of the Problem

If these challenges are significant within an organization, they may lead to several consequences.

For members:

Additional time spent searching for information.

Delays in completing tasks or making decisions.

Difficulty learning from experienced colleagues or community members.

Repeated dependence on a small number of knowledgeable individuals.

Uncertainty about whether a question has been answered adequately.

For experienced contributors:

Repeatedly answering similar questions.

Valuable expertise remaining confined to limited conversations.

Difficulty sharing knowledge with a wider group of members.

For the organization or community:

Loss of useful knowledge when people leave or change roles.

Reduced discoverability of previous solutions and lessons.

Difficulty identifying frequently encountered problems.

Missed opportunities to improve knowledge sharing and collaboration.

These consequences are potential impacts, not yet established findings. Interviews, surveys, and observations should be used to determine which occur most frequently and how seriously they affect the target organization.

7. Existing Gap and Opportunity

Organizations may already use messaging applications, discussion forums, shared documents, knowledge bases, or question-and-answer platforms to exchange information.

These tools can be valuable, and some already support several functions relevant to DoubtChain. Therefore, the project should not assume that existing platforms are incapable of resolving doubts.

Instead, the opportunity is to investigate whether a unified workflow can make it easier to:

Submit and categorize doubts.

Discover relevant previous questions and answers.

Connect people seeking guidance with experienced contributors.

Preserve useful answers and practical experiences.

Track a question's progress from submission to resolution.

Identify unanswered or recurring questions.

Confirm whether the person asking the question considers it resolved.

The extent to which existing tools already meet these needs must be established through research and comparison before the project's differentiation can be claimed.

8. Proposed Solution: DoubtChain

DoubtChain is proposed as a peer-to-peer doubt-resolution platform designed to improve knowledge discovery, collaboration, and resolution tracking within organizations and communities.

The platform would provide a structured environment where members can ask questions, share experiences, offer solutions, and follow the status of a doubt.

Its proposed workflow is as follows:

Raise a doubt: A member submits a question with a clear description and relevant category.

Discover existing knowledge: Members search for related doubts, answers, or previously shared experiences.

Contribute knowledge: Other members provide explanations, suggestions, and experience-based solutions.

Continue the discussion: The person asking the question can request clarification when an answer is incomplete.

Track progress: The doubt moves through appropriate statuses, such as Open, In Progress, and Resolved.

Confirm resolution: The person who raised the doubt confirms when it has been adequately addressed.

Preserve knowledge: The question and its useful responses remain available for future members, subject to the organization's access and retention policies.

The intended value is to make peer knowledge easier to access and reuse while providing visibility into the progress of unresolved questions.

9. Initial Project Scope

The initial Minimum Viable Product (MVP) should focus on the essential functionality required to test whether structured peer-to-peer doubt resolution provides value.

Core features:

Create and view doubts.

Categorize and search doubts.

View existing questions and answers.

Submit responses and clarifications.

Track doubt status.

Mark a doubt as resolved with confirmation from its creator.

Preserve resolved discussions for future reference.

Advanced capabilities, such as AI-assisted question matching, automatic expert recommendations, analytics dashboards, reputation mechanisms, and integrations with organizational systems, can be evaluated after the core workflow has been validated.

10. Problem Statement

Within organizations and communities, members frequently encounter doubts and practical challenges for which relevant knowledge or experience may already exist among other members. However, this knowledge can be fragmented across communication channels, difficult to discover, dependent on personal networks, or insufficiently preserved for future use. Informal discussions may also provide limited visibility into whether questions have received satisfactory answers or remain unresolved.

There is an opportunity to develop a structured peer-to-peer doubt-resolution platform that enables members to raise questions, discover existing knowledge, share experiences, collaborate on solutions, and track doubts through to resolution. Such a platform aims to improve the accessibility, continuity, and reuse of knowledge within an organization or community.

11. Assumptions Requiring Validation

The following assumptions should be investigated before finalizing the project's requirements:

Members experience meaningful difficulty finding answers or locating people with relevant experience.

Useful organizational knowledge is currently distributed across multiple channels or informal interactions.

Members are willing to contribute answers and share practical experiences.

Searching existing questions can reduce unnecessary repetition.

Explicit resolution tracking provides value beyond ordinary discussion threads.

Members and administrators consider the proposed platform useful enough to adopt.

These assumptions can be tested through interviews, surveys, observation of existing workflows, and feedback from an early prototype.

12. Expected Outcome and Evaluation

DoubtChain aims to make organizational knowledge easier to discover, encourage peer-to-peer assistance, and improve visibility into unresolved questions.

The MVP should be evaluated using measurable indicators, such as:

Time taken to find a relevant answer.

Percentage of submitted doubts receiving responses.

Percentage of doubts confirmed as resolved.

Time between submitting a doubt and receiving a useful response.

Frequency of repeated questions.

User satisfaction and willingness to reuse the platform.

Baseline measurements should be collected where possible, and results should be compared against the organization's existing process. These indicators are proposed evaluation metrics; actual improvements must be demonstrated through testing rather than assumed.

The long-term objective is to help organizations and communities transform individual knowledge and experience into a more accessible, reusable, and collaborative knowledge resource.
