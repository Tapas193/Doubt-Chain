Problem Statement Selection Matrix

DoubtChain — Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution Tracker

1. Introduction

Selecting an appropriate problem statement is an important step in project development. A good problem statement should address a meaningful user need, offer scope for a practical solution, be feasible to implement with available resources, and provide opportunities for measurable improvement.

For DoubtChain, the selection process focuses on identifying a problem related to knowledge sharing, collaboration, and the resolution of questions within organizations and communities.

This document defines the selection criteria, compares potential problem areas, and presents a preliminary justification for selecting the problem that DoubtChain aims to address.

2. Objective of the Selection Matrix

The objective is to evaluate potential problem statements systematically rather than selecting an idea based only on personal preference or technical interest.

The evaluation considers:

The significance of the problem and its potential impact on users.

The likelihood that the intended users experience the problem.

The feasibility of building and testing a solution.

The opportunity to introduce meaningful improvements over existing approaches.

The ability to measure the solution's effectiveness.

The possibility of extending the solution to additional users or organizations.

The selection matrix provides a transparent framework for comparing alternatives and documenting the rationale behind the final decision.

3. Candidate Problem Statements

The following candidate problem areas are used for the preliminary comparison. They are illustrative alternatives and should be replaced or refined if the project team evaluated a different set of problem statements.

PS-A: Organizational Knowledge Discovery

Members of an organization may struggle to locate relevant information because knowledge is distributed across documents, conversations, and individual experiences.

PS-B: Task and Project Coordination

Teams may experience difficulties tracking responsibilities, deadlines, task progress, and dependencies when coordinating collaborative work.

PS-C: Organizational Event Coordination

Organizations may face challenges coordinating participants, sharing event information, managing registrations, and communicating updates across different groups.

PS-D: Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution and Knowledge Reuse

Members may struggle to find appropriate guidance, ask questions, access relevant experiences, and determine whether their doubts have received satisfactory answers. Useful knowledge may remain fragmented or difficult to reuse.

DoubtChain focuses on PS-D, which combines knowledge discovery with a structured process for resolving and preserving questions.

7. Interpretation of the Results

Based on the preliminary matrix, the candidates rank as follows:

PS-D — Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution: 4.15/5

PS-A — Organizational Knowledge Discovery: 3.95/5

PS-B — Task and Project Coordination: 3.85/5

PS-C — Organizational Event Coordination: 3.45/5

PS-D receives the highest preliminary score because it combines a potentially meaningful organizational need with a feasible initial implementation, identifiable evaluation metrics, and opportunities to preserve knowledge for future use.

Its scope also supports a focused MVP: users can submit doubts, search existing discussions, contribute answers, and track resolution. This makes it possible to test the central workflow without implementing a large organizational management system.

PS-A is closely related to PS-D. The distinction is that general knowledge discovery focuses primarily on finding information, whereas DoubtChain additionally emphasizes asking questions, receiving responses, continuing discussions, and tracking whether the original doubt has been resolved.

PS-B addresses an established coordination need but has a different central objective: managing work rather than resolving knowledge gaps. PS-C is relatively feasible to implement, but the preliminary assessment suggests a narrower opportunity for differentiation in the proposed project context.

These comparisons are provisional. The rankings should not be presented as objective proof that PS-D is universally superior to the alternatives.

8. Justification for Selecting DoubtChain

The selection of DoubtChain is supported by five preliminary considerations.

8.1 A clear problem to investigate

The project addresses the possibility that useful knowledge exists within an organization but is difficult for other members to access when they need help.

8.2 A defined set of users

The primary users are members seeking guidance, while experienced members contribute answers and practical knowledge. Administrators and moderators may help maintain a useful environment.

8.3 A manageable initial scope

The first version can focus on doubt submission, search, responses, and resolution tracking. More advanced features can be considered after validating the core workflow.

8.4 Measurable outcomes

The project can be evaluated using response rates, resolution rates, time to receive a useful answer, repeated-question frequency, and user satisfaction.

8.5 Potential for broader application

The same core workflow may be useful in educational institutions, corporate teams, technical communities, and other organizations. Each setting would require appropriate testing and potentially different access controls.

9. Key Risks and Limitations

The preliminary selection also involves uncertainties that should be addressed before development progresses.

Existing alternatives: Discussion platforms, knowledge bases, and question-and-answer systems may already provide some of the proposed functionality.

User participation: The platform will be useful only if members are willing to ask questions and contribute helpful answers.

Answer quality: A response does not necessarily mean that a doubt has been resolved.

Adoption: Users may prefer their existing communication channels unless the new workflow provides clear value.

Evidence quality: The current scores have not yet been validated through user research.

These risks should inform the literature review, comparison of existing solutions, and MVP testing plan.

10. Validation Plan

Before treating the selection as final, the project team should:

Confirm the actual candidate problem statements considered by the team.

Interview members from the intended organization or community.

Identify how questions are currently asked, answered, and tracked.

Investigate existing tools and determine which needs remain unmet.

Review the preliminary criteria and weights with the project team.

Update the ratings based on collected evidence.

Record the final rationale for selecting the problem statement.

Where possible, each score should be accompanied by supporting evidence, user feedback, or a documented technical assessment.

11. Final Selection

Selected problem statement: Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution and Knowledge Reuse within Organizations and Communities.

DoubtChain is selected provisionally because it offers a focused opportunity to investigate knowledge accessibility, peer collaboration, and resolution tracking within a manageable software project.

The proposed solution aims to help members ask questions, discover relevant existing knowledge, share experiences, and track doubts until they are adequately resolved.

The selection will be considered validated when user research and comparison with existing approaches provide sufficient evidence that the problem is meaningful and that the proposed workflow offers practical value.

12. Conclusion

The preliminary selection matrix provides a structured rationale for prioritizing DoubtChain over three illustrative alternative problem areas. Its main strengths are a clearly defined user workflow, a feasible initial implementation, and the potential to evaluate whether questions are answered and resolved more effectively.
