Research and Literature Review

DoubtChain — Peer-to-Peer Doubt Resolution Tracker

1. Introduction

Knowledge sharing is an important part of learning, collaboration, and organizational development. Members of an organization acquire knowledge through formal training, personal experience, collaborative work, and interactions with other people.

However, possessing knowledge within a community does not automatically mean that every member can access it when needed. Practical experience may remain with individuals, useful discussions may be difficult to retrieve, and members may encounter challenges when trying to obtain guidance from others.

DoubtChain investigates how a structured peer-to-peer platform could help members ask questions, share knowledge and experiences, discover previous answers, and track doubts until they are resolved.

This literature review examines research on organizational knowledge sharing, barriers to knowledge transfer, online question-and-answer communities, answer quality, and the preservation of organizational knowledge. These areas provide a foundation for understanding the problem and identifying opportunities for further investigation.

2. Research Objectives

The objectives of this literature review are:

To understand the importance of knowledge sharing within organizations and communities.

To investigate barriers that prevent people from accessing or sharing useful knowledge.

To examine how online question-and-answer platforms facilitate peer-to-peer assistance.

To understand why answer quality and user participation matter in collaborative knowledge-sharing systems.

To investigate the importance of preserving knowledge and practical experience for future use.

To identify potential research gaps that can guide DoubtChain's design and MVP evaluation.

3. Literature Review

3.1 Knowledge Management and Knowledge Sharing

Study 1: A Systematic Review of Knowledge Management and Knowledge Sharing: Trends, Issues, and Challenges

Authors: Muhammad Asrar-ul-Haq and Sadia Anwar, with Tahir Nisar as reviewing editor.

Publication: Cogent Business & Management, 2016.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2015.1127744

Research focus

This review examined 64 articles published between 2010 and 2015 to investigate factors that facilitate or hinder knowledge management and knowledge sharing.

Key findings

The review discusses the importance of individual, organizational, and technological factors in knowledge sharing. It also identifies challenges associated with transferring practical, experience-based knowledge, which may be difficult to capture in a structured form.

Relevance to DoubtChain

This research supports investigating whether organizational knowledge is easy to discover and transfer between members. It also suggests that creating a platform alone may not be sufficient: organizational culture, willingness to contribute, and the usability of the sharing process may influence its effectiveness.

3.2 Knowledge Sharing Processes and Outcomes

Study 2: Knowledge Sharing in Organization: A Systematic Review

Author: Asiamah Yeboah.

Publication: Cogent Business & Management, 2023.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2023.2195027

Research focus

The study reviewed 110 articles to examine knowledge-sharing enablers, processes, and outcomes.

Key findings

The review identifies several interconnected dimensions of knowledge sharing, including the conditions that encourage participation, the processes through which knowledge is exchanged, and the outcomes that follow. It also highlights the importance of connecting knowledge-sharing practices with organizational objectives and resources.

Relevance to DoubtChain

DoubtChain should not evaluate success merely by counting how many doubts or answers are submitted. It should also examine whether members receive useful assistance, whether doubts are resolved, and whether the resulting knowledge can help other members later.

This supports the inclusion of measurable outcomes in the MVP, such as response rates, resolution rates, and user satisfaction.

3.3 Barriers to Organizational Knowledge Sharing

Study 3: Three-Dozen Knowledge-Sharing Barriers Managers Must Consider

Publication: Journal of Knowledge Management, 2005.

Link: https://www.emerald.com/jkm/article-abstract/9/3/18/261773/Three-dozen-knowledge-sharing-barriers-managers

Research focus

This paper reviews knowledge-management literature to identify a broad range of potential barriers to knowledge sharing.

Key findings

The paper groups potential barriers into individual, organizational, and technological categories. These categories indicate that knowledge sharing may be affected by personal willingness, organizational practices, and the tools used to communicate or preserve information.

Relevance to DoubtChain

This research provides a useful framework for investigating why members may not ask questions or share their knowledge.

For example, members may be reluctant to ask questions publicly, may not know where to post them, or may prefer existing communication channels. These possibilities should be tested with intended users rather than treated as established facts about every organization.

DoubtChain should therefore prioritize a straightforward question-submission process and an environment that encourages constructive participation.

3.4 Online Question-and-Answer Communities

Study 4: A Comparative Assessment of Answer Quality on Four Question Answering Sites

Author: Pnina Fichman.

Publication: Journal of Information Science, 2011.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1177/0165551511415584

Research focus

The study compared the quality of answers across four online question-and-answer services.

Key findings

The study found differences in answer accuracy, completeness, and verifiability across the services. It also reported that receiving multiple answers did not automatically improve answer accuracy.

Relevance to DoubtChain

This finding is important because the number of responses alone cannot demonstrate that a doubt has been resolved.

A useful platform should distinguish between receiving a response and receiving a satisfactory response. DoubtChain can address this by allowing the person who asked a question to request clarification and confirm when the doubt has been resolved.

Where appropriate, the platform may also need moderation or reporting mechanisms to address misleading or inappropriate responses.

3.5 Motivation to Contribute Answers

Study 5: Why Users Keep Answering Questions in Online Question Answering Communities: A Theoretical and Empirical Investigation

Authors: Xiao-Ling Jin, Zhongyun Zhou, Matthew K. O. Lee, and Christy M. K. Cheung.

Publication: International Journal of Information Management, 2013.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2012.07.007

Research focus

The study investigated factors associated with users continuing to answer questions in online question-and-answer communities.

Key findings

The research examines continued participation in knowledge-sharing communities through factors related to knowledge-sharing motivation and users' confidence in their ability to contribute knowledge.

Relevance to DoubtChain

DoubtChain depends on participation from people who are willing and able to help others. Even a well-designed system may struggle if experienced members do not contribute answers.

The MVP should therefore make it easy to respond to questions and recognize helpful contributions without assuming that users will participate simply because the platform exists.

Possible approaches, such as contributor recognition or notifications for relevant unanswered questions, should be tested before adding them as core features.

3.6 Knowledge Transfer and Organizational Memory

Study 6: Procedures for Transferring Organizational Knowledge During Generational Change: A Systematic Review

Publication: Heliyon, 2024.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e27092

Research focus

This systematic review examined procedures used to transfer organizational knowledge, including knowledge-transfer practices associated with changes in personnel and organizational knowledge-management initiatives.

Key findings

The review identified knowledge retention, digitalization, communication practices, and collective approaches to knowledge transfer as important themes in the literature.

Relevance to DoubtChain

When experienced members move to different roles or leave an organization, some of their practical knowledge may become harder to access if it has not been preserved.

DoubtChain could help retain useful knowledge by keeping resolved questions, explanations, and experience-based contributions accessible to authorized community members.

However, storing conversations alone does not guarantee that their contents will remain useful. Searchability, organization, content quality, and appropriate access controls are also important considerations.

3.7 Knowledge Sharing and Tacit Knowledge

Study 7: Tacit Knowledge in Organizations: Bibliometrics and a Framework-Based Systematic Review

Authors: Asha Thomas and Vikas Gupta.

Publication: Journal of Knowledge Management, 2022.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1108/JKM-01-2021-0026

Research focus

This review examines research on tacit knowledge, including its antecedents, outcomes, theories, methods, and potential directions for future research.

Key findings

Tacit knowledge is an important area of organizational knowledge research. It includes forms of understanding that develop through personal experience and may be difficult to communicate or formalize completely.

Relevance to DoubtChain

Some useful answers involve more than factual information. Experienced members may need to explain how they approached a situation, what mistakes they encountered, and why they chose a particular solution.

DoubtChain could provide a place for members to document such experiences alongside direct answers to specific questions.

This is a proposed application of the research, not evidence that a particular platform design will necessarily improve knowledge transfer.

4. Synthesis of the Literature

The reviewed studies suggest several themes relevant to DoubtChain.

4.1 Knowledge availability is different from knowledge accessibility

An organization may contain useful information and experienced people, yet members may still have difficulty locating the knowledge they need. This makes knowledge discovery an important area to investigate.

4.2 Knowledge sharing depends on people and processes

Technology is only one part of knowledge sharing. Participation, trust, motivation, organizational practices, and ease of use can also affect whether members contribute and reuse knowledge.

4.3 Answer quantity does not guarantee answer quality

A question receiving several responses is not necessarily resolved. The quality, relevance, completeness, and usefulness of those responses matter.

4.4 Experience-based knowledge deserves attention

Practical knowledge gained through experience may be difficult to capture in conventional documentation. Structured discussions and experience-sharing contributions may provide one way to preserve some of this knowledge.

4.5 Knowledge preservation requires more than storage

Information must be discoverable and useful to future users. Organizing questions, categorizing contributions, enabling search, and retaining resolution status may help, but their effectiveness must be evaluated.

4.6 User participation is central to the proposed system

The value of a peer-to-peer platform depends on people contributing useful questions and answers. The MVP must test whether the proposed workflow encourages this behavior.

5. Research Gap and Opportunity for DoubtChain

The reviewed literature provides a foundation for understanding knowledge sharing, online question-and-answer communities, answer quality, and organizational knowledge transfer.

However, the reviewed sources do not, by themselves, establish that all existing platforms have a specific missing feature or that DoubtChain would outperform them.

The following are therefore proposed areas for investigation, rather than proven gaps in the entire research field.

5.1 Connecting questions with reusable experience

Investigate whether members can easily discover previous answers and practical experiences relevant to their current doubts.

5.2 Making resolution status visible

Investigate whether a structured progression from an open doubt to a confirmed resolution provides value beyond ordinary discussion threads.

5.3 Distinguishing responses from satisfactory resolutions

Investigate whether allowing the question creator to confirm resolution helps users distinguish between a question that received a reply and one that was actually addressed.

5.4 Reducing repeated questions

Investigate whether searchable, categorized discussions help members discover previous solutions before submitting similar questions.

5.5 Understanding participation within a defined community

Investigate what encourages members to share knowledge, answer questions, and return to the platform.

5.6 Evaluating practical outcomes

Investigate whether a structured doubt-resolution workflow reduces the time required to find useful guidance or improves users' reported satisfaction.

These investigation areas provide a basis for defining DoubtChain's MVP and evaluating it with actual users.

6. Implications for MVP Design

The literature suggests that the initial MVP should focus on testing the core knowledge-sharing and resolution workflow.

The first version should avoid unnecessary complexity. Advanced features such as artificial intelligence, automatic expert matching, elaborate reward systems, and predictive analytics should be considered only after the basic workflow has been tested.

7. Proposed Evaluation Method

The MVP should be evaluated with members of a defined organization or community.

The evaluation can include:

User interviews: Understand current methods of asking questions and finding guidance.

Workflow observation: Identify difficulties with existing communication and knowledge-sharing practices.

Prototype testing: Ask users to submit doubts, find existing answers, contribute responses, and mark doubts as resolved.

Usage measurement: Track submissions, responses, resolution status, and time to receive useful answers.

User feedback: Measure perceived usefulness, ease of use, satisfaction, and willingness to continue using the platform.

Possible metrics include the percentage of doubts receiving responses, the percentage confirmed as resolved, median time to a useful response, and the frequency of repeated questions.

Baseline data should be collected where feasible. Results should be interpreted in the context of the size of the test group, duration of testing, and type of questions submitted.

8. Limitations of the Literature Review

This document is an initial, focused literature review rather than an exhaustive systematic review.

The studies cover different populations, contexts, platforms, and research methods. Their findings cannot automatically be generalized to every organization or community.

The review also does not establish the current market capabilities of competing products. That requires a separate comparison of existing solutions.

Before the final submission, the project team should verify the bibliographic details, access the full text of relevant studies where possible, and expand the search with additional primary research directly related to doubt tracking, knowledge discovery, and question-resolution workflows.

9. Conclusion

The reviewed literature establishes a useful research foundation for investigating peer-to-peer knowledge sharing, barriers to knowledge transfer, answer quality, contributor motivation, and organizational knowledge preservation.

These themes align with DoubtChain's proposed goal of helping members ask questions, find relevant knowledge, share practical experiences, and track doubts through to resolution.

The literature does not prove that DoubtChain is unique or that it will necessarily improve organizational outcomes. Instead, it helps identify the questions the project should test: whether members experience a meaningful problem, whether the proposed workflow improves knowledge discovery and resolution tracking, and whether users find sufficient value to adopt it.

The next stage is to compare existing solutions, validate the proposed research gaps, and evaluate the MVP against clearly defined user needs and measurable outcomes.
